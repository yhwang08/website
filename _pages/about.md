I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://www.lbl.gov/). I am also affiliated with the [International Computer Science Institute (ICSI)](https://icsi.berkeley.edu/), where I am part of the Deep Learning Group. My research lies at the intersection of hydrology and scientific machine learning, with a focus on developing reliable, physically consistent, and interpretable models for hydrology and Earth system science.

My work includes:

- Modern deep learning for hydrology: advancing architectures such as state-space models and diffusion-based generative models for streamflow simulation and probabilistic forecasting.
- Physics-aware neural networks: embedding mass-conservation principles into neural networks to study how physical constraints affect predictive performance, generalization, and learned representations across diverse hydroclimatic conditions.

My goal is to develop deep learning methods that more effectively integrate hydrologic knowledge, and to foster collaboration between the hydrologic science and artificial intelligence communities. More broadly, I want to build accurate and reliable modeling tools for understanding and predicting water-system behavior, supporting hazard preparedness and informed water-resources decision-making.

I received my Ph.D. from the University of Oklahoma, my M.S. from the University of California, Los Angeles, and my B.E. from Hohai University.

A full list of my publications is on [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=MkTGi1QAAAAJ). I'm always happy to talk about research and collaborations. The best way to reach me is by email.
